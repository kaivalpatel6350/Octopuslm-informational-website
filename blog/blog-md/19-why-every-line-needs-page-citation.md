# Why Every Line in a Chronology Needs a Page Citation

**Category:** Trust Posts — Your Strongest Theme  
**Target Audience:** IME Physicians, Legal Nurse Consultants, Medical Record Reviewers  
**Estimated Read Time:** 9 minutes

---

## The Citation Problem Nobody Talks About

You've built a chronology. It's 50 lines long. It covers 400 pages of medical records. You're proud of it. It's clear, organized, and tells a story.

Then opposing counsel asks: "Where did you find that the plaintiff reported pain 8/10 on February 15th?"

You look at your chronology. You see the entry: "2024-02-15: Plaintiff reports pain 8/10."

But you don't have a page citation. You can't answer the question.

**This is the problem. And it's more common than anyone admits.**

---

## Why Citations Matter

### **1. Defensibility**

Every claim in your chronology must be verifiable. If you can't point to the source, the claim is indefensible.

**Example:**
- Your chronology: "MRI shows disc herniation."
- Opposing counsel: "Which MRI? What date? What page?"
- Without a citation, you can't answer.

### **2. Credibility**

If one claim in your chronology is wrong, every claim is questioned.

**Example:**
- Your chronology: "Plaintiff treated with Dr. Smith from January to March."
- Opposing counsel: "Actually, Dr. Smith's records show treatment from February to April."
- Now, every other entry in your chronology is suspect.

### **3. Efficiency**

When you're cross-examined, you need to find the source quickly. Citations let you do that.

**Example:**
- Opposing counsel: "What's the basis for your opinion that the plaintiff had a pre-existing condition?"
- You: "See chronology entry dated 2019-06-15, citing family doctor note at page 45."

### **4. Professionalism**

A chronology without citations looks amateur. It looks like you didn't do your homework.

---

## What a Good Citation Looks Like

### **Format:**

**Basic format:**
- "Page [number]" (if using original page numbers)
- "Bates [number]" (if using Bates numbering)
- "Tab [number], page [number]" (if using tabs)

**Example:**
- "Page 45"
- "Bates 0045"
- "Tab 3, page 12"

### **What to cite:**

**For each entry, cite:**
- The page number where the information is found
- The specific document (if not clear from context)

**Example:**
- "2024-02-15: Plaintiff reports pain 8/10. (Dr. Smith progress note, p. 45)"

## Common Citation Mistakes

### **1. No Citation at All**

**The mistake:** You list the finding but don't cite the source.

**Example:**
- "2024-02-15: Plaintiff reports pain 8/10."

**The fix:** Add the citation.
- "2024-02-15: Plaintiff reports pain 8/10. (Dr. Smith progress note, p. 45)"

---

### **2. Citation to the Wrong Page**

**The mistake:** You cite a page, but the information is on a different page.

**Example:**
- "2024-02-15: Plaintiff reports pain 8/10. (p. 45)"
- But the progress note is actually on page 47.

**The fix:** Verify every citation before finalizing the chronology.

---

### **3. Citation to a Multi-Page Document Without Specificity**

**The mistake:** You cite a multi-page document without specifying the page.

**Example:**
- "2024-02-15: MRI shows disc herniation. (MRI report, p. 50-55)"

**The fix:** Cite the specific page where the finding is stated.
- "2024-02-15: MRI shows disc herniation at L4-5. (MRI report, p. 52)"

---

### **4. Citation to a Summary, Not the Original**

**The mistake:** You cite a summary (e.g., a family doctor note summarizing a specialist report) instead of the original.

**Example:**
- "2024-02-15: Specialist recommends surgery. (Dr. Smith note, p. 45)"
- But the specialist report is on page 60-65.

**The fix:** Cite the original source.
- "2024-02-15: Specialist recommends surgery. (Orthopedic consult, p. 62)"

---

### **5. Citation to a Duplicate**

**The mistake:** You cite a duplicate record instead of the authoritative version.

**Example:**
- "2024-02-15: ER report. (p. 312)"
- But page 312 is a duplicate. The authoritative version is on page 1-8.

**The fix:** Cite the authoritative version.
- "2024-02-15: ER report. (p. 1-8, duplicate at p. 312)"

## How to Build a Chronology with Citations

### **Step 1: Use a Standardized Format**

Create a template for your chronology:

| Date | Provider | Record Type | Finding | Citation | Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|---------|----------|-------|
| 2024-02-15 | Dr. Smith | Progress Note | Pain 8/10 | p. 45 | |
| 2024-02-20 | ABC Physio | Treatment Note | ROM improved | p. 52 | |

---

### **Step 2: Enter the Citation Immediately**

When you enter a finding, enter the citation at the same time. Don't go back later to add citations — you'll forget.

---

### **Step 3: Verify Every Citation**

Before finalizing the chronology, verify every citation:
- Go to the cited page
- Confirm the finding is there
- Confirm the citation is accurate

---

### **Step 4: Use Bates Numbers if Available**

If the records have Bates numbers, use them. They're more precise than original page numbers.

---

### **Step 5: Note Duplicates**

If you cite a duplicate, note it:
- "ER report (p. 1-8, duplicate at p. 312)"

---

## What This Looks Like in Practice

### **Before (chronology without citations):**

| Date | Finding |
|------|---------|
| 2024-02-15 | Pain 8/10 |
| 2024-02-20 | ROM improved |
| 2024-02-25 | Returned to work |

**Problem:** No citations. Can't verify.

---

### **After (chronology with citations):**

| Date | Provider | Record Type | Finding | Citation | Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|---------|----------|-------|
| 2024-02-15 | Dr. Smith | Progress Note | Pain 8/10 | p. 45 | |
| 2024-02-20 | ABC Physio | Treatment Note | ROM improved, pain 4/10 | p. 52 | |
| 2024-02-25 | Employer | Return-to-Work Form | Returned to modified duties | p. 78 | Restrictions: No lifting over 10 lbs |

**Better:** Every entry is cited. Every claim is verifiable.

## The Cross-Examination Scenario

**Opposing counsel:** "You state that the plaintiff reported pain 8/10 on February 15th. Where did you find that?"

**You (with citations):** "That's in Dr. Smith's progress note, dated February 15, 2024, at page 45 of the records."

**You (without citations):** "I believe it was in one of the progress notes. I'd need to check."

**Which answer is more credible?**

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Every line in a chronology needs a page citation.**
2. **Citations provide defensibility, credibility, efficiency, and professionalism.**
3. **Use a standardized format: Date, Provider, Record Type, Finding, Citation, Notes.**
4. **Enter citations immediately, not later.**
5. **Verify every citation before finalizing.**
6. **Use Bates numbers if available.**
7. **Note duplicates explicitly.**
