# What to Do When Records Arrive Out of Order and Mid-Page

**Category:** Their Gaps — Nobody Covers These  
**Target Audience:** IME Physicians, Legal Nurse Consultants, Medical Record Reviewers  
**Estimated Read Time:** 8 minutes

---

## The Messy File Problem

You've received the records. You open the PDF and:

- Page 1 is the middle of a progress note (starts mid-sentence)
- Page 2 is an ER report from a different date
- Page 3 is a physiotherapy note
- Page 4 is the first page of the progress note from page 1
- Page 5 is a duplicate of page 2

**The file is out of order. Pages are cut mid-stream. Duplicates are mixed in.**

This is more common than anyone admits. Insurance adjusters, paralegals, and medical records departments compile files quickly. They don't verify order or completeness. You inherit the mess.

**This post shows you how to handle it.**

---

## Why This Happens

### **1. Multiple Sources**

Records come from different providers. Each sends their own packet. The compiling party merges them without reordering.

**Result:** ER notes, family doctor notes, and physiotherapy notes are interleaved randomly.

### **2. Scanning Errors**

Paper records are scanned in batches. Pages get misfed. The scanner captures half a page. The next page starts mid-document.

**Result:** Pages are cut off or out of order.

### **3. EMR Printouts**

EMRs print records in reverse chronological order by default. But some providers print chronologically. The compiling party doesn't notice.

**Result:** Date order is inconsistent.

### **4. Manual Compilation**

A paralegal manually compiles records from multiple sources. They don't verify order. They staple and scan.

**Result:** Random order.

---

## The Problems This Creates

### **1. Chronology Confusion**

You can't build a chronology if you don't know the order of events.

### **2. Missed Context**

A note that starts mid-page may reference a prior note. You miss the connection.

### **3. Duplicate Confusion**

You see the same content twice. You don't realize it's a duplicate. You count it as two events.

### **4. Citation Errors**

You cite page 1, but page 1 is the middle of a note. The citation is meaningless.

---

## How to Handle Out-of-Order Records

### **Step 1: Don't Panic**

This is fixable. It takes time, but it's fixable.

### **Step 2: Create a Page Inventory**

Go through the file page by page. For each page, note:

| Page # | Provider | Date | Record Type | Notes |
|--------|----------|------|-------------|-------|
| 1 | Dr. Smith | 2024-01-20 | Progress Note (p. 2 of 2) | Starts mid-sentence |
| 2 | City ER | 2024-01-15 | ER Report | |
| 3 | ABC Physio | 2024-01-22 | Initial Assessment | |
| 4 | Dr. Smith | 2024-01-20 | Progress Note (p. 1 of 2) | First page of note on p. 1 |
| 5 | City ER | 2024-01-15 | ER Report | Duplicate of p. 2 |

**This inventory becomes your map.**

### **Step 3: Reconstruct the Logical Order**

Using your inventory, reconstruct the logical order:

1. Identify complete documents (e.g., "Progress note, 2 pages")
2. Match fragments (e.g., "Page 1 is p. 2 of 2, page 4 is p. 1 of 2")
3. Sort by date

**Logical order:**

| Logical Page | Original Page | Provider | Date | Record Type |
|--------------|---------------|----------|------|-------------|
| 1 | 2 | City ER | 2024-01-15 | ER Report |
| 2 | 5 | City ER | 2024-01-15 | ER Report (duplicate) |
| 3 | 4 | Dr. Smith | 2024-01-20 | Progress Note (p. 1 of 2) |
| 4 | 1 | Dr. Smith | 2024-01-20 | Progress Note (p. 2 of 2) |
| 5 | 3 | ABC Physio | 2024-01-22 | Initial Assessment |

### **Step 4: Build Your Chronology in Logical Order**

Use the logical order, not the original page order. Cite both:
- "Progress note (logical p. 3-4, original p. 4, 1)."

### **Step 5: Note the Disorder in Your Report**

Be transparent:
- "Records were received out of order. Pages were reorganized chronologically for this review. Original page numbers are cited alongside logical page numbers."

## How to Handle Mid-Page Cuts

### **Step 1: Identify the Cut**

A page starts mid-sentence. Note it:
- "Page 1 starts mid-sentence: '...continued to report pain in the cervical region.'"

### **Step 2: Find the Missing Part**

Search for the preceding page:
- Look for a page that ends mid-sentence
- Look for a page from the same provider on the same date
- Look for a page that logically precedes the cut

### **Step 3: Reconstruct the Document**

If you find the missing part, note it:
- "Page 1 is the second page of a progress note. The first page is page 4."

### **Step 4: Note If You Can't Find It**

If you can't find the missing part, note it:
- "Page 1 starts mid-sentence. The preceding page was not provided. Content may be incomplete."

---

## How to Handle Duplicates Mixed In

### **Step 1: Identify Duplicates**

Use content-matching (see our post on [Duplicate Records: Why Header-Matching Fails](#)).

### **Step 2: Flag Them**

In your inventory, note duplicates:
- "Page 5 is a duplicate of page 2."

### **Step 3: Choose the Authoritative Version**

Determine which version to cite:
- Most complete
- Best quality
- Has additional content (e.g., handwritten addenda)

### **Step 4: Note in Chronology**

"ER report (original p. 2, duplicate at p. 5)."

---

## The Workflow That Works

### **Phase 1: Inventory (30-60 minutes)**

- Go through every page
- Note: provider, date, record type, notes
- Flag: mid-page cuts, duplicates, out-of-order

### **Phase 2: Reconstruct (30-60 minutes)**

- Match fragments
- Identify complete documents
- Sort by date

### **Phase 3: Build Chronology (60-120 minutes)**

- Use logical order
- Cite both logical and original page numbers
- Note disorder in report

### **Phase 4: Verify (30-60 minutes)**

- Check that all pages are accounted for
- Check that all fragments are matched
- Check that all duplicates are flagged

---

## What This Looks Like in Practice

**Scenario:** You receive a 200-page file. Pages are out of order. Some start mid-page.

**Approach:**
1. Spend 30-60 minutes creating a page inventory.
2. Spend 30-60 minutes reconstructing the logical order.
3. Build your chronology in logical order.
4. Note the disorder in your report.

**Result:** A defensible chronology, despite the messy file.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Out-of-order and mid-page records are common. Don't panic.**
2. **Create a page inventory. Note provider, date, record type, and issues for each page.**
3. **Reconstruct the logical order. Match fragments. Sort by date.**
4. **Build your chronology in logical order. Cite both logical and original page numbers.**
5. **Note the disorder in your report. Be transparent.**
6. **Flag duplicates. Choose the authoritative version.**
7. **If you can't find a missing part, note it. Don't assume.**
