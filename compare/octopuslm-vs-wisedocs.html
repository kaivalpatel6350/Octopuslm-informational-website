OctopusLM vs Wisedocs — Medical Chronology Software Compared

Both turn medical records into chronologies and summaries for claims, legal, and IME work. They are built for different buyers. Here is an honest comparison so you can pick the right one.

The short version

Wisedocs is an enterprise claims-documentation platform sold to insurance carriers, TPAs, and large IME and legal organizations. It pairs AI with human expert oversight, offers API integrations into claims systems, and is sold through a sales process — there is no public pricing and no self-serve sign-up.

OctopusLM is a self-serve web application for the professional who has to read the file today. Upload PDFs, get a narrative summary and dated chronology with a page citation on every line, export to Word or Excel. Pricing is public — $0.10 per page or $250 per month unlimited — and teams can add multi-user accounts on a custom Insurance & Legal plan.

If you are a carrier standing up a programme-wide, integrated documentation platform with a services component, evaluate Wisedocs. If you are an IME physician, legal nurse consultant, adjuster, or claims unit that wants a defensible chronology on a real file this afternoon without a procurement cycle, start with OctopusLM.

Where OctopusLM is the better fit

  • You want to run a file now. No demo, no contract, no minimum. The first file costs cents per page.
  • You need to verify, not trust. Every line in the chronology cites its page. That is the property that makes an AI chronology defensible when opposing counsel or an appeals tribunal asks where a fact came from. See why every line needs a page citation.
  • You are a solo or small practice. IME physicians, legal nurse consultants, and small firms are the core user base, and the pricing is built for them.
  • You work Canadian files. SABS, OCF forms, LAT, WSIB, and CNESST are covered in depth, not as a footnote.
  • You want predictable cost. $250 a month unlimited means the tenth file costs the same as the first.

Where Wisedocs is the better fit

  • You are buying for the whole carrier. Wisedocs is positioned as a claims decision-intelligence platform with automated workflows, co-mingled record detection, and enterprise integrations.
  • You want a services layer. Its model includes expert human oversight of the AI output, which some organizations require contractually.
  • You need API integration into a claims management or document management system as part of the initial rollout.
  • Procurement needs a SOC 2 Type II report today.

What both tools agree on

Both companies say the same thing about AI in claims: it should organize the record and make it searchable, and humans should make the decisions. Where they differ is who the human is — Wisedocs supplies expert oversight as part of the service; OctopusLM gives you page citations so the verification is yours, fast, and free. Frequently asked questions

Is OctopusLM a Wisedocs alternative?

For individual professionals and claims or legal teams that want a self-serve, page-cited chronology at published pricing, yes. For enterprise programmes that need integrations and a human-review services layer, Wisedocs and OctopusLM are solving different problems.

Does Wisedocs publish pricing?

Not on its public website as of this writing. OctopusLM's pricing is public: $0.10 per page, $250 per month unlimited, and a custom Insurance & Legal plan for teams.

Can I use OctopusLM without talking to sales?

Yes. Sign up, upload a file, and pay per page. Sales is only involved if you want the multi-user Insurance & Legal plan.

Which is more accurate?

Neither company can honestly answer that for your files without running them. What we can say is that every OctopusLM chronology line cites its source page, so accuracy is something you can check in minutes rather than take on faith.

Does OctopusLM offer human review of the output?

No. OctopusLM is software; the professional using it does the verification, which the page citations make fast. If you need a vendor to supply reviewers, that is a services purchase and Wisedocs positions itself there.
