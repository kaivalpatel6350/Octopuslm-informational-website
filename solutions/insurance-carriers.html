The problem: the medical file is the bottleneck

On a bodily injury, disability, or workers' compensation claim, the medical records are usually the largest, slowest, and most consequential part of the file. They arrive as scanned PDFs, out of order, with duplicates, handwriting, and mixed providers. Someone has to read all of it before reserves are set, causation is assessed, or an IME is scoped.

That someone is expensive — a nurse case manager, a medical director, an outside review vendor — and the wait is measured in days or weeks.

What OctopusLM does with a claim file

Upload the PDFs exactly as you received them. OctopusLM reads every page and produces:

  • A narrative medical summary — mechanism of injury, diagnoses, treatment course, pre-existing conditions, current status.
  • A dated medical chronology — every encounter, provider, diagnosis, medication, imaging result, and work-status note in order.
  • A page citation on every line. Click any entry and the source page opens. Nothing is asserted without a reference back to the record.
  • Word and Excel export for the claim file, the IME referral, or defence counsel.

Because every line is cited, the output is something a claims professional can verify in minutes rather than trust on faith. That is the difference between an AI summary that is useful in a contested claim and one that is not.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from a medical record summary vendor?

A summary vendor returns a document written by a person days later, and you take it at face value. OctopusLM returns a chronology in minutes where every line links to the page it came from, so your own nurse reviewer or adjuster can verify the parts that matter. Most carriers use it to triage and to prepare files before an outside review, not to replace medical judgement.

Does OctopusLM work for Canadian claims?

Yes. It processes Ontario SABS files, OCF-18 and OCF-23 forms, WSIB documentation, and CNESST records, and the platform is PIPEDA compliant. The blog has detailed guides on each of these jurisdictions.

Can it handle very large or badly scanned files?

Yes. Files with thousands of pages, out-of-order records, mid-page breaks, duplicates, and handwritten notes are the normal case, not the exception. Where OCR cannot read a page confidently, the output flags it rather than guessing.

What does it cost for a claims team?

Individual users pay $0.10 per page or $250 per month unlimited. Team plans with multi-user accounts, case sharing, and volume rates are priced per program — book a 15-minute demo and we will scope it against your file volume.

Is there an API or claims-system integration?

Team plans are scoped individually and integration needs are part of that conversation. Start with a pilot on exported PDFs; most teams find the Word and Excel export fits the existing file workflow immediately.
