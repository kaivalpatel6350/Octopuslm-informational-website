Why MVA and BI files break manual chronologies

A motor vehicle accident file is the hardest routine file to summarize by hand. The claimant sees the ER, a family doctor, a physiotherapist, a chiropractor, a massage therapist, an orthopaedic surgeon, a pain clinic, and a psychologist — each producing records in its own format, arriving in whatever order the custodian scanned them, with the pre-accident history mixed in. Building a reliable timeline from that takes hours; getting it wrong changes the value of the claim. See why multi-provider MVA files break chronologies.

Frequently asked questions

How does OctopusLM handle records from many different providers?

Every page is read and every encounter is placed on one timeline by date, regardless of provider or the order the records were scanned. Duplicates — a consult that appears in both the family doctor's file and the specialist's — are identified by content, not header text.

Does it work with Ontario SABS and OCF forms?

Yes. OCF-18, OCF-23, and other HCAI forms are read alongside the clinical records, and their contents appear as dated entries in the chronology. The blog has detailed guides on SABS requirements, OCF forms, and LAT hearings.

Can it separate pre-accident and post-accident history?

The chronology is dated, so pre-accident records naturally sit before the date of loss and post-accident records after. The summary also calls out documented pre-existing conditions explicitly, with citations.

Is the output usable for a mediation brief or a LAT hearing?

Yes. Export to Word or Excel and the page citations carry through, so anyone reading the brief can check the source page for every asserted fact.

Is it a desktop tool?

No. OctopusLM is a web application — nothing to install. Records are encrypted in transit and at rest, processed in HIPAA- and PIPEDA-compliant infrastructure, and never used to train models.
