Disability files are a different kind of hard

A disability claim — short-term, long-term, or SSDI — is decided on the relationship between documented medical findings and functional capacity over time. The file is not one injury and six months of treatment; it is often several conditions, a dozen providers, and years of notes in which the important statements about function are buried in routine follow-ups.

The questions are specific:

  • What are the diagnosed conditions, and when was each established?
  • What does the record say about functional capacity — sitting, standing, lifting, concentration, attendance — and how has it changed?
  • Is treatment ongoing and compliant, and is there a documented plateau?
  • Are there gaps in treatment or inconsistencies between providers?
  • Does the record support the own-occupation / any-occupation transition at the change-of-definition date?

Long treatment histories and mental health records

Two file types dominate disability work and both are hard to summarize by hand:

  • Chronic pain and musculoskeletal — years of physio, injections, medication changes, and imaging. See chronic pain claims and long treatment histories.
  • Psychiatric and psychological — narrative-heavy notes where symptom severity, medication response, and functional statements are scattered. See reviewing mental health documentation.

OctopusLM is built for both, and for files where the two overlap.

Frequently asked questions

Does OctopusLM decide whether someone is disabled?

No. It organizes and cites the medical record so that the examiner, physician, or adjudicator making the decision can see the full functional picture quickly and point to the exact pages that support it. The determination remains a human judgement.

How does it handle files that span many years?

Long files are the normal case. OctopusLM processes thousands of pages, orders every encounter by date regardless of how the records were scanned, and identifies duplicates by content so a re-sent consult does not inflate the treatment history.

Can it pull out functional capacity statements specifically?

Functional statements — restrictions, limitations, work-status notes, activities of daily living — appear as dated entries in the chronology alongside the clinical findings that accompany them, with the source page cited.

Is it suitable for SSDI as well as private LTD?

Yes. The chronology and summary are the same regardless of the benefit type; the file is the file. Representatives and consultants working SSDI claims use the same workflow as insurer disability units.

Is the data handled securely?

Records are encrypted in transit and at rest, processed in HIPAA- and PIPEDA-compliant infrastructure, and never used to train models. OctopusLM is a web application; nothing is installed locally.
